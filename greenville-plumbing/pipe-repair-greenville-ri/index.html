    One isolated leak on sound pipe calls for a repair. Repeated leaks are different. If you have galvanized, polybutylene or copper that keeps pinholing, replacing the system often costs less than a few years of patching. You get both numbers.

    Freezing is a leading cause. Frozen pipes are a regular winter risk in Rhode Island, especially in exterior walls, crawlspaces and unheated areas of older homes. High pressure plays a part. Static pressure above about 80 psi, corrosion on older pipe, a settling foundation and damage from nails or screws all weaken pipe until it splits or a fitting blows apart.     Corrosion from the inside of the pipe. Water chemistry drives it. High velocity, excess pressure and stray electrical current from poor grounding wear the pipe from within until it pins through.     A bill that climbs for no reason is a big one. Discolored water counts. Water stains, persistent damp spots, the sound of water running when nothing is on and a sudden drop in pressure all point to a leak you cannot see yet. The meter will confirm it.
