    What Our Tankless Techs Fix in Greenville, RI

    Commercial Tankless Repair in Greenville, RI

    Hotels and gyms draw hot water from open to close, so their tankless racks scale up two or three times faster than a home unit. We service rack systems with manifold kits, pulling the fault log and testing the flow sensor on every heater in the rack, and set a descaling schedule by run hours. Quarterly is common.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Greenville, RI

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. Undersized gas pipe is the one people miss. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Recirculation Pump Repair in Greenville

    Recirculation cuts the wait for hot water at a far bathroom from a minute or more to a few seconds. When the pump fails or the check valve sticks, you get long waits again or lukewarm water in the cold line. We test the pump, look for a thermal bypass valve under the farthest sink, and set the schedule so it runs around your mornings and evenings. Two settings, mostly.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Greenville, RI

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. Clogged traps shut the unit down. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Repair in Greenville, RI

    Noritz heaters hold up well, but their flow sensors and vent connections still wear out. A code 90 points to combustion trouble, often a dirty fan or a restricted intake. We clean the fan and burner, check the vent joints for gaps, and test the flow sensor's output against the spec sheet. Gaskets get replaced if they leak.

    Tankless Repair vs Replacement Cost in Greenville, RI

    Descaling usually runs $175 to $350, and sensor or igniter repairs land between $250 and $600. A heat exchanger out of warranty can cost $800 to $1,500 in parts and labor. Replacement is a bigger number. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.

    Electric Tankless Element Repair in Greenville, RI

    Electric units fail in their own way. Stiebel Eltron and EcoSmart models use two to four heating elements in separate chambers, and one burned element means weak heat at high flow, while a failed flow switch means no heat at all. We check each element's resistance, measure voltage under load and check amperage on each leg, then confirm the panel and wire size match the unit's rating.

    Tankless Flush & Scale Removal in Greenville

    Lukewarm water at high flow and a unit that runs loud are two early signs of scale. Well-done installs usually include isolation valves with purge ports for this, and if yours does not, we can add them during the visit so future flushing takes less time. Vinegar works in a pinch. Commercial descaler works faster.
