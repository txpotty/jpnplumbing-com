    What We Fix on Toilets in Greenville, RI

    Fill Valve Replacement in Greenville

    Old ballcock valves with a brass arm and a big plastic float are loud and slow to refill, and they tend to stick open when scale builds up in the ports. We swap them for an adjustable fill valve from Fluidmaster or Korky and set the height so the critical level mark sits at least an inch above the overflow. It is a twenty-minute job. You will notice the difference the first night, because the tank stops hissing.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Repair in Greenville

    Restaurant restrooms get the hardest use we see, and wall-hung bowls there can loosen on their carriers until the seal leaks behind the wall. We tighten or rebuild the carrier and reseal the bowl. That one needs care. Leave a loose wall-hung bowl alone long enough and the china can crack at the mounting holes.

    Toilet Gasket and Seal Replacement in Greenville, RI

    The rubber parts in a toilet, the flapper, the tank-to-bowl gasket, the bolt washers and the wax ring at the floor, all break down with age and chlorine. They get stiff. A stiff gasket stops sealing and lets water seep out slowly where you will not see it until the floor stains. When one tank seal has failed, we replace the full set so the others do not follow next month.

    Cracked Tank and Supply Line Repair in Greenville, RI

    Braided supply lines last years, but the plastic nut on the toilet end can crack and a bulge in the braid means the inner hose is about to burst. We check every supply line on every visit. Bulges get replaced on the spot. Old vinyl or solid plastic lines get swapped for braided stainless with metal nuts.

    Emergency Toilet Repair in Greenville, RI

    An overflowing bowl in a one-bathroom house is an emergency, and so is a burst supply line spraying water across the floor at full house pressure. Shut the angle stop first. Turn the valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it is tight, then call us so we can walk you through the rest. The first goal on site is stopping the water, then a permanent repair if the part is on the truck.

    Running Toilet Repair in Greenville

    A tank that keeps hissing and refilling every few minutes is almost always losing water past the flapper or overfilling into the overflow tube. It adds up fast. We check the chain, the float height, the flapper and the seat before touching a part, then replace only what failed. Figure on thirty minutes for a typical running toilet, longer if the flush valve seat is pitted.
