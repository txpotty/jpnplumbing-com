    Whole-Home Copper Repiping in Newport East

    Copper is the traditional choice, and Type L copper has a thicker wall than the Type M that went into many older homes. Type L tolerates UV, stays rigid and has decades of field history. It costs more. We sweat joints with lead-free solder or press them, hang the pipe at proper support intervals and test the whole system before patching.

    Kitec and Polybutylene Replacement in Newport East

    Kitec, an orange or blue PEX-AL-PEX pipe with brass fittings, and gray polybutylene are both known for failures. Both still turn up in older homes. We can usually identify it from the pipe at the water heater, and full replacement makes the house easier to insure and sell.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Newport East, RI

    Older homes may still have galvanized steel pipe that rusts from the inside out. Weak flow and rusty water are common. We remove or abandon the galvanized lines and replace them with PEX or copper, and your water pressure and water quality usually improve the same day the new lines go into service.

    Copper Repiping in Newport East, RI

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. Our copper work uses thicker Type L pipe, joined with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings, rather than the Type M found in many older houses. Expect a slightly longer job. Most copper jobs run a day longer than PEX, since each turn needs its own fitting.

    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Newport East

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs. We use Uponor or similar PEX-A with cold expansion fittings that open the pipe end and let it shrink back over the fitting for a full-bore joint. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. Two or three days covers most houses.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Newport East

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. We can reroute a single hot or cold branch overhead and abandon the leaking pipe in the slab, which costs far less than a repipe and avoids breaking the floor. It suits a first-time leak. After multiple leaks, we will show you why a full repipe may cost less over the next ten years.
