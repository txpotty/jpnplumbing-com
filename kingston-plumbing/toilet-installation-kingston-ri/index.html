    Kinds of Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    High-efficiency toilets flush on 1.28 gallons or less, against 3.5 gallons or more for toilets from before the early 1990s. Check for the WaterSense label. We steer customers toward models with a MaP score of 800 grams or higher, because a cheap low-flow bowl that needs two flushes saves nothing. Dual-flush models add a lighter half flush.

    ADA-Compliant Toilet Installation in Kingston

    ADA toilets put the seat 17 to 19 inches off the floor, and the centerline sits 16 to 18 inches from the side wall. It is set that way so a grab bar fits. Before anything is ordered we measure the drain, because shifting it to meet the ADA centerline means cutting into the floor. Elongated bowls are standard here.

    Commercial Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    Commercial restrooms take a beating, so we install vitreous china bowls with flushometer valves rather than tank toilets. Flushometers want a 1 inch supply line and steady pressure. Before setting fixtures in an office or shop, we check that the building supply can deliver the flow the valve is rated for, often 20 to 25 gallons per minute at the fixture. Fixture counts follow code.

    Commercial Restroom Toilets in Kingston, RI

    Restaurants and offices get heavy-duty fixtures and accessible stalls built to code. Downtime costs a business money. We schedule commercial installs early in the morning or after hours, and we stage the fixtures and valves on site so each stall is down for as short a time as possible. We work with your GC on inspections.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    Wall-hung toilets need a wall at least 2 by 6 deep, or a furred-out chase built in front of an existing 2 by 4 wall. Floors stay clear underneath. That makes mopping easier and lets you set the bowl height anywhere within the carrier's range, which is handy for tall households or anyone who wants a comfort-height seat without a tall bowl. Service happens through the flush plate.

    Smart Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    Smart toilets with heated seats and built-in bidets need two things the old toilet did not: power and a dedicated water connection. Most need a GFCI outlet close enough for the cord. We run the water side and mount the unit, and if a new outlet is needed we bring in a licensed electrician rather than splicing anything ourselves. Then we test every button.

    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Kingston, RI

    Comfort height bowls stand about two inches taller than standard ones. Consider who uses the bathroom first. Shorter adults and kids sometimes find the taller seat leaves their feet dangling, while anyone with bad knees or a hip replacement usually finds it much easier to sit and stand. Installation is the same as a standard bowl.

    Standard Toilet Installation in Kingston

    A standard floor-mount toilet is a two-hour job when the flange is sound. The stop gets shut, the tank drained, and the old bowl lifted out. After scraping the old wax off, we check the flange for cracks, set new stainless bolts, and lower the new toilet straight down onto a fresh seal so it compresses evenly. The old one leaves with us.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Installation in Kingston

    Most homeowners in Kingston, RI pay $200 to $500 to have a toilet installed, fixture not included. Removing a stubborn old unit adds time. Bolts rusted solid have to be cut with a hacksaw or oscillating tool, and a cast iron flange that breaks during removal needs a repair ring, each of which adds labor.

    Both seal fine on a flange set at the right height. Wax has decades behind it. Wax-free foam and rubber seals survive being lifted and reset, and they hold up better over heated floors. We pick based on your flange height.

    About two hours for most floor-mount toilets. Wall-mounted and smart toilets take longer. A wall-mounted unit needs its carrier framed and plumbed before the wall is finished, so it often spans two visits, and a smart toilet may wait on an electrician for the outlet.

    Adding or moving a toilet almost always needs one. A simple swap usually goes without. The permit covers drain sizing and venting, and the inspector wants to see both before the floor or wall is closed, so we schedule the inspection around your remodel timeline.

    Two warranties apply. The manufacturer covers the toilet. Most brands give one to five years on parts like the fill valve and flapper and a longer term, sometimes lifetime, on the vitreous china, while our own guarantee covers any leak traced back to how we set it.

    Cracks in the tank or bowl are the clearest sign. Hairline cracks can be hard to spot. Look for a slow drip on the floor that comes back after you dry it, or frequent clogs on an early low-flow model with a narrow trapway. So is a toilet you have rebuilt twice in three years.

    Figure one to two hours for a standard floor-mount toilet. Some jobs run a full day. Wall-hung systems and drain relocations on a slab mean opening walls or floors, so those get scheduled as their own project with time for patches to cure.

    Homeowners set basic toilets all the time. Flange height and bolt torque are where it goes wrong. A low flange needs an extender ring, and one extra turn on a nut can split the base, which is why new floors and wall-hung units should go to a pro.
