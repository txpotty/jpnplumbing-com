    What Our Repipe Crews Do in Kingston, RI

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Kingston, RI

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. We replace the full system with PEX or copper. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Kingston, RI

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Brown water after the house sits overnight, weak flow at upstairs showers and leaks at threaded joints are the usual signs that galvanized is near the end. New PEX or copper takes its place. Owners usually notice stronger flow the first day.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Kingston

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. Clogged aerators are a clue. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest. We disconnect and abandon the old pipe where removing it would mean tearing out finished walls.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Kingston, RI

    Blue-green stains on sinks and tubs are a frequent sign of corrosive water, and treatment can protect the plumbing. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. In hard water homes, PEX-A is often the better pick since it resists scale, and a softener loop or filter can be added during the job. Planning it now saves a second visit later.

    Type L Copper Repiping in Kingston

    Copper has been plumbing homes for generations, and done right it lasts a long time. Our copper repipes use Type L with proper hangers and isolators so the pipe does not wear against framing. Lead-free solder only. Because some water chemistries eat copper, we look at your leak history before recommending it over PEX.

    Commercial Repipe Services in Kingston

    Older commercial buildings in Kingston often have galvanized risers, copper that has pinholed or plastic pipe that no longer meets code. Our crews swap out risers, branches and drains with as little interruption to tenants as the building allows. Tenants get notice of every shutoff. Work is phased by zone, and we coordinate access, keys and noise limits with the property manager.

    Residential Repiping Plans in Kingston

    Every house is piped a little differently, so a repipe starts with a walk-through. Our estimator counts fixtures, checks access, finds the main and asks about any planned remodels before choosing a route. Then we size the lines. Bigger homes may need a 1-inch trunk so the shower does not drop when the dishwasher fills.

    PEX Repiping in Kingston, RI

    PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run long distances without a fitting, which means fewer joints hidden in your walls. Fewer joints, fewer leaks. PEX shrugs off scale and chlorine, survives freezing better than copper and installs faster, so it is our most common repipe material. Our PEX-A installs use expansion fittings that do not shrink the flow path. Flow stays strong at the last shower.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Kingston

    Most PEX repipes for Rhode Island homes cost $5,000 to $12,000, based on size, bathroom count and access. Copper typically adds 20 to 40 percent.

    Copper is rigid metal pipe with a long track record. PEX bends instead. PEX is cheaper, quicker and quieter and handles freezes better, while copper tolerates sun and heat and suits owners who want metal, so it depends on your house.

    Repeated slab leaks, pinhole leaks, low pressure, discolored water or polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized pipe are all signs. Repeat repairs say the most.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most Rhode Island jurisdictions. We pull the permit and book the inspections.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. Uponor PEX-A is our usual PEX. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper lasts but is pricier and can pinhole in corrosive water.

    Most repipes in Kingston, RI take three to five working days for an average house. Big houses take more time. Copper takes a little longer than PEX because every turn needs a fitting, and the drywall patching usually adds a final day after the plumbing passes inspection.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX is cheaper. PEX bends, resists scale and handles freezes better, and copper is rigid, UV-tolerant and proven but pricier and vulnerable to aggressive water.
