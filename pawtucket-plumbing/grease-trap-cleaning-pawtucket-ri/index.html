    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. The 25 percent rule shows up in most of them. Plenty also cap the interval, want a year or more of records on site, and ban enzymes in place of pumping, so we look up your program before we schedule.

    Zurn, Schier, Watts and Big Dipper units turn up across Rhode Island every week, along with Rockford traps and older steel boxes from makers that are long gone. We can usually find parts.     It depends on trap size and menu. Most restaurants end up on a two-week to three-month interval. Homes with a small unit may go a year, but a fryer-heavy kitchen on a 50-gallon trap might need pumping every other week.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. The records are your responsibility. Installing a new trap or interceptor, or changing the size of one, usually needs a plumbing permit and sometimes a pretreatment program review before the work starts.

    Skimming off the top layer yourself is usually allowed. Disposing of it is where people get in trouble. Pretreatment programs often require a licensed hauler's record for each pump-out, so a kitchen that handles its own cleaning may have no paperwork to show when the inspector asks.
