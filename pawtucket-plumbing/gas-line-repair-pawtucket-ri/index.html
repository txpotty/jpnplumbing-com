    Permits and Code Compliance for Gas Work in Pawtucket, RI

    Most gas piping work beyond a simple connector swap needs a permit in Pawtucket, RI. The inspector checks pipe sizing, material, support spacing, shutoff locations, sediment traps and the pressure test result before signing off. We handle the paperwork. A permitted repair also gives you a paper trail for insurance and for any future sale.

    Gas Appliance Connector Repair in Pawtucket, RI

    Weak flames or a smell near one appliance often point to the connector or shutoff behind it. Uncoated brass connectors from decades ago crack at the flare fittings and should be replaced on sight. We replace them with coated stainless connectors. Each appliance also gets a shutoff you can reach and a sediment trap where required, and we check that the connector length and diameter match the appliance rating.

    Appliance Connector and Shutoff Repair in Pawtucket

    Old flexible connectors, especially uncoated brass ones, crack and leak with age. We replace them with new coated stainless connectors sized for the appliance and add an accessible shutoff valve if the old one is missing or seized. Usually under an hour per appliance. Furnaces, water heaters, ranges and dryers each need a sound connection, and most need a drip leg before the appliance valve.

    Propane Gas Line Service in Pawtucket, RI

    Propane systems run at different pressures than natural gas and use a two-stage regulator setup between the tank and the house. Regulators fail too. We repair buried and above-ground propane lines, replace leaking regulators and fittings and check that each appliance has been converted with the correct orifices for propane. Wrong orifices make a burner run dirty and unsafe. We test the system the same way we test natural gas piping.

    Gas Permits and Inspections in Pawtucket

    Repairs that change piping, and all new gas lines, usually need a permit and inspection from Pawtucket building officials. We file the permit, schedule the inspection and are on site when the inspector checks the work and the test gauge. Inspectors often come within the week. Once it passes, the utility can restore service and we come back to relight appliances if needed.
