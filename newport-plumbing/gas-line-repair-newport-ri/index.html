    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Repair in Newport

    Tightening or resealing a connection often runs $150 to $400. Section replacements cost more. After-hours emergencies or longer pipe replacements usually land between $500 and $2,500, depending on access, material and how much pipe is involved.

    Leaks at the meter or on the utility's service line belong to the gas company. Leave the meter alone. Get out of the house, call the utility's emergency line, and once they have handled their side, we can repair any leaks on the house side of the meter, including the riser and the yard line.

    There is no required yearly inspection for most homes. A look every few years helps older houses. It is worth a check if your home has old uncoated brass connectors, buried steel yard lines from decades ago or appliances that are burning with yellow or lazy flames, since those are common warning signs.

    Simple repairs take an hour or two. Yard line replacements usually take a day, and permitted work adds inspection time.

    Look for dead or yellow grass in a line across the yard, bubbling in wet soil, a hissing sound or a rising gas bill. Any of those can point to a buried leak.

    Most standard repairs, like fixing a small leak or replacing a short section, are done in a few hours. Relocations take more time. A long replacement or a reroute can take a day or need a second visit, and we tell you the expected timeline after the inspection.

    We repair outdoor lines for grills, fire pits, pool heaters and patio heaters. Weather is hard on them. Exposed pipe takes a beating from sun, irrigation and mowers, so we use coated steel or buried PE and confirm the sizing.

    We work on black iron, coated steel, CSST such as Gastite and TracPipe, and polyethylene for buried lines. Code decides where each one goes. Polyethylene can only run underground and needs a listed riser where it comes up, while CSST needs bonding and black iron needs proper support and gas-rated thread sealant.
