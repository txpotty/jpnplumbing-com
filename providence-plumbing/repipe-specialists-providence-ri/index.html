    PEX Pipe Replacement in Providence, RI

    PEX tubing resists freezing damage, corrosion and scale better than most metal pipe. It is quieter too. Flexible PEX soaks up some of the pressure spike behind water hammer, and its long fitting-free runs leave fewer weak points. We keep PEX out of sunlight and away from water heater flues.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Providence

    PEX repipes in Providence commonly run $4,000 to $15,000. Copper costs more. Copper repipes often fall between $8,000 and $25,000 or more, and the biggest factors are the size of the house, the number of bathrooms and fixtures and how easy the attic or crawlspace is to work in. You get an itemized quote at the visit.

    A documented repipe removes a known risk that buyers and inspectors look for. Buyers notice it. Swapping out polybutylene, Kitec or galvanized makes a home easier to insure and sell, especially with a permit record and transferable warranty.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. Copper jobs often get Viega ProPress fittings.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX is cheaper. PEX is flexible, handles a freeze better and resists scale, while copper is rigid, tolerates sunlight and has a long history, but it costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    Our repipes come with a written workmanship warranty on parts and labor, usually covering several years. Manufacturer coverage adds more. Certified PEX-A installs carry the manufacturer's long-term warranty on the pipe and fittings, and the exact terms of both warranties are on your estimate.

    Water in Rhode Island is generally soft and can be slightly acidic, which may cause pinhole leaks in copper pipe over time. Hard water deposits calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe over time. Pressure falls as scale builds. In metal pipe, scale also speeds up corrosion, so repiping with PEX-A and adding a softener or filter during the job can protect the new plumbing and your fixtures for years.

    Rusty or brown water, low pressure at several fixtures and repeated pinhole leaks are the classic signs. Noisy pipes can be another. If your home has galvanized steel, polybutylene or Kitec, a repipe is often worth planning before a failure forces the issue, since those materials tend to fail across the whole house rather than in one spot.
