    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Providence, RI

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. Plan on roughly an hour for the swap. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.

    Power Vent Water Heater Repair in Providence

    Power vent heaters push exhaust through PVC with a blower on top of the tank. The usual failures are the blower motor, the pressure switch and the hot surface igniter. Condensate pooling in a vent with the wrong slope can also trip the pressure switch, so we check the pipe run all the way to the termination before we sign off on a part. The control's error code is our starting point. Then each part gets a meter test.

    All Major Gas Water Heater Brands Serviced in Providence

    Every brand label we see in garages and closets uses one of a handful of gas control designs. Rheem and Ruud share most parts, and State shares a lot with A.O. Smith. Bradford White runs its own control system, so we stock those separately. Knowing which family a heater belongs to lets the tech bring the right valve, thermocouple or igniter on the first trip instead of ordering parts and coming back two days later. A photo of the rating plate helps us prep.

    Flammable Vapor Sensor Repair in Providence

    Since about 2003, most residential gas tanks have been built with a flammable vapor ignition resistant combustion chamber. Air enters through a flame arrestor screen at the base that also keeps flame inside. When lint, dust or pet hair coats that screen, the burner starves, the chamber overheats and a thermal switch shuts the whole heater down, sometimes several times a week. The screen and the chamber both get cleaned. A tripped thermal switch gets replaced.

    Pilot Light Repair in Providence

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. With a millivolt meter, it takes about a minute to see if it is putting out enough. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Gas Water Heater Code Compliance in Providence, RI

    Fuel gas code covers more of a water heater repair than most people expect. The tech looks for a drip leg, a listed flex connector and an accessible shutoff valve. Garage installs in Providence, RI need the burner raised 18 inches off the floor unless the heater is listed as vapor resistant, and strapping goes on wherever the local code asks for it. Clearances get measured too.

    Bradford White & Rheem Gas Water Heater Repair in Providence, RI

    Bradford White and Rheem tanks make up a big share of what we repair in Providence, RI. Rheem gas models mostly use White-Rodgers or Honeywell controls with a status light. Bradford White uses its own sealed combustion design with a lint screen at the base and a thermal switch, and it needs a specific kit when the pilot assembly goes bad. Both kits ride on the truck. Using factory parts keeps your tank warranty valid.

    Pilot Light and Burner Issues in Providence

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.
