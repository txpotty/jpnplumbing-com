    Walls, Slabs and Yards: Leak Detection in Providence, RI

    PEX and Copper Leak Detection in Providence, RI

    Copper leaks are loud on a ground microphone. PEX is quieter, since plastic pipe does not carry sound well, so we lean more on thermal scans, moisture readings and tracer gas when the house is plumbed in it. We handle both every week. Pinholes in copper and failed crimp fittings in PEX are the two leaks we find most.

    Main Water Line Leak Detection in Providence

    Soggy grass near the front walk or a sinking spot over the line can mean the service pipe is leaking. Old galvanized and copper lines fail most. We map the pipe with a line tracer and listen for the leak with a ground microphone, so excavation stays to one small hole instead of a trench across the yard. Repair options follow.

    Hidden Wall and Ceiling Leaks in Providence

    Bubbling paint, soft drywall and a musty smell are the signs we hear about most. All of them mean water. Thermal cameras find the wet area, and a moisture meter confirms it. Acoustic tools help when the leak is on a pressurized line, and once the source is pinned down we open one small access hole rather than following the stain around the room.

    Gas Line Leak Locating in Providence

    Gas leaks are found by test, not by nose. Our plumbers pressure test the system with a gauge, and check every joint with soap and a detector. We also walk the yard line, since a dead strip of grass over a buried gas pipe often marks a leak that has been there a while. We coordinate with the gas utility.

    Slab Leak Signs in Providence, RI

    Warm floor spots, cracked tile and a meter that never stops are the common signs. So is the sound of water with everything off. We isolate hot and cold lines with pressure tests, then trace and listen through the slab, then lay out the spot repair, reroute and repipe choices with a price on each.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Providence, RI

    A brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. Our job is figuring out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe before any big opening is made.

    Pool Leak Detection in Providence

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation probably has a leak. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, use an underwater hydrophone to listen, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain so we can point to the exact fitting or crack.
