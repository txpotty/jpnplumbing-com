    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Providence

    PEX-B is stiffer, is cheaper by the roll, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. A crimp joint is only as good as the tool, so we check every ring with a go/no-go gauge, and a ring that does not pass gets cut out and redone. It is a five-second check. Skipping it is how leaks start.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Providence, RI

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorinated water breaks the pipe and its plastic fittings down from within, which means it can look perfectly fine until a fitting suddenly fails. Many insurers ask whether you have it. Replacing it with PEX can make the house easier to insure and sell.

    PEX Repipe Cost Comparison in Providence, RI

    Price on a repipe depends on a short list: bathroom count, square footage, how many stories, and how easy the attic and walls are to work in.     PEX-A, PEX-B, and PEX-C Systems in Providence, RI

    There are three grades of PEX, named for how the plastic is cross-linked at the factory. PEX-A is the most flexible and joins with expansion rings, PEX-B is stiffer and joins with crimp rings or cinch clamps, and PEX-C sits closer to B in feel. All three are code-listed for drinking water. Pipe, fittings and tools come from the same maker on our jobs, which keeps the warranty valid.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Providence

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Both last decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.
