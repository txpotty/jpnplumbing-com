    Batch-Feed Units for Kitchens Without a Switch in Providence

    Batch-feed disposals only run when the stopper lid is in place, which adds safety for families with kids. They need no wall switch, so they are a good choice for kitchens where adding a switch would mean cutting into finished drywall or tile backsplash. Mounting and drains are standard.

    Batch-Feed Disposal Installation in Providence, RI

    A batch-feed disposal will not start until the lid is in, and the lid holds a magnet that closes a switch inside the unit. No wall switch is needed, which makes them handy in kitchens where running a new switch would mean opening finished walls. They are slower to use. Families with small kids often like the trade.

    Standard Disposal Installation in Providence

    Most households in Providence do well with a 1/3 to 1/2 horsepower continuous-feed unit. We remove the old unit if there is one, set a new flange in fresh plumber's putty, hang the disposal on a new mount and connect it to the trap with a new discharge tube and gasket. Then we wire it and test it. We listen for a smooth spin and watch the drain clear.

    InSinkErator Model Selection in Providence, RI

    We help you pick between the Badger and Evolution lines based on how you cook, how much noise bothers you and how much cabinet room you have.     InSinkErator Disposal Installation in Providence, RI

    InSinkErator uses the Quick Lock mount on most current models, with a ring we snug down using the wrench in the box. Badger units are the simple single-stage line, while Evolution models add grind stages and insulation that make them noticeably quieter in an open kitchen. Cords are often sold separately.     Commercial Disposal Installation in Providence

    Commercial disposals run 1 to 5 horsepower and typically come with a control panel and solenoid valve and need their own circuit. We set the unit on the mounting collar, run the drain to the grease trap where the local rules require it, and wire the controls so the water runs whenever the motor does. Health codes vary.     High-Horsepower Disposal Installation in Providence

    A 3/4 or 1 HP unit is bigger and heavier than a starter model, so we measure the cabinet first. Taller units can push the discharge outlet below the wall stub, and when that happens we either lower the drain at the wall or pick a model with a higher outlet so water does not sit in the chamber. We also check the circuit. A larger motor pulls more current when it starts.

A plug left in backs up the dishwasher. An air gap or high loop goes in too, which keeps sink water from running back into the dishwasher.
