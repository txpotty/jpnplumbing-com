    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Providence

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. Basement homes can cost more when fixtures sit below the sewer and need an ejector pump. We price gas piping as its own line. A square-foot guess misses too much, so we bid from plans.

    It matters more than most owners expect. A correction notice needs a quick return visit. A plumber based an hour away may not come back until next week to add a missing strap, which can hold up your insulation inspection and every trade after it.

    Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. The underground inspection happens before the slab is poured, with drains under a water or air test. Top-out gets inspected before insulation, and the final comes after fixtures are set and running. Some add a gas test.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. The under-slab work runs one to three days. Top-out usually takes three to five days on a single-family house, and trim takes one or two days once cabinets, counters and tile are in place. Inspections add a day each.

    We do, mostly on custom homes. We work from your architect's plans and suggest changes that shorten runs or improve flow. Stacking baths, moving the water heater closer to the main bath and adding a recirculation line are the usual ones. Small moves save pipe.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling fixtures or sewer odor point to venting or slope problems. We can camera the line from the cleanout to look for a belly or a bad joint while the builder warranty still applies. Construction debris is common.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drain, waste and vent runs are PVC or ABS. Cast iron is still used in some multifamily buildings and in walls next to bedrooms where drain noise matters, since it is much quieter than plastic when a toilet flushes upstairs.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in Rhode Island homes. It handles heat well and resists the scale and pinhole problems copper can get with aggressive water. We run drains in Schedule 40 PVC. Under slabs, we keep joints to a minimum and use long-sweep fittings at every change of direction so a cable or camera can get through later.
