    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Providence

    Gas tankless units in Providence are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. Gas is the catch. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Tankless Water Heater Replacement in Providence, RI

    Replacing an old tankless unit is usually simpler than a first-time conversion. Since the gas line and vent are already there, most swaps mean matching connections, inspecting the old vent and mounting the new heater. Vent sizes can change by brand. We check before ordering.

    Recirculation Systems for Tankless Heaters in Providence

    Tankless heaters can take a few extra seconds to get hot water to a far bathroom, because the unit has to sense flow and fire first. A recirculation pump on a timer or a motion sensor keeps the hot line primed, so the wait at the farthest sink drops from a minute to a few seconds. It saves water too.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Providence, RI

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and parts are easy to get. Their condensing and non-condensing lines both fit different homes, depending on the vent route and the budget you are working with. Non-condensing units vent in stainless. Condensing models use PVC vent and a condensate drain.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Providence

    Upgrading from a tank in Providence starts with a look at what you have now: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits. In some homes the old flue gets capped and the new unit vents out a side wall in PVC, which is simpler and opens the old chase. Other homes need a longer run. We spell that out on the quote.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Providence, RI

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A typical 40-gallon gas tank uses around 40,000 BTU, while a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the gas line from the meter almost always gets upsized. The vent changes as well. We plan both on the quote.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Providence

    Conversions in Providence tend to run a full workday. We drain and remove the tank, upsize and test the gas, run a new vent through a wall or roof, then mount, plumb and fire the unit with service valves. You shower that night. Inspection follows within days.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Providence

    Propane homes in Providence can run tankless units too, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. Tank size matters too.
