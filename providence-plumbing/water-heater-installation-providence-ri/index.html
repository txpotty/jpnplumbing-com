    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Providence, RI

    Going tankless means the heater fires only when a tap opens, and it never runs out. Gas tankless models usually need a bigger gas line than the old tank had, plus a dedicated vent run through the wall or roof. They cost more to install. We check gas capacity before quoting.

    Water Heater Expansion Tank Installation in Providence

    Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume water gains when it heats. Local inspectors review permitted work, and JPN Plumbing handles the permit and inspection steps so the job follows code. An expansion tank gives that water somewhere to go, so the relief valve stops dripping and pipe joints stop taking pressure spikes every time the burner runs. We pre-charge it to your house pressure. It mounts on the cold inlet.

    Gas Water Heater Installation in Providence, RI

    Gas heaters recover faster than electric, often refilling a 50 gallon tank in under an hour. Our crews set the gas line, the drip leg and the vent connector, then do a draft test at the hood to prove the exhaust is leaving the house. Then we check for leaks. We add an expansion tank if code calls for it.

    Electric Water Heater Installation in Providence, RI

    Electric tanks need a 240-volt circuit, usually a 30-amp double-pole breaker with 10-gauge wire. Before hooking up the new tank we check that the breaker and wire size match the elements, since a mismatch is a common cause of tripped breakers and scorched terminals. No venting needed. That makes placement easier.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Providence, RI

    A pressure reducing valve or check valve on the main turns your plumbing into a closed system. Heated water then has no way back to the street, so pressure rises and works on the relief valve, supply lines and toilet fill valves. The tank soaks up that extra volume. We pre-charge it to match your water pressure.

    Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Providence

    Tank heaters still heat most homes' water, holding 40 to 75 gallons at temperature until you need it. We size the tank by first-hour rating, not just gallons, because that number tells you how much hot water it delivers in a busy morning. Either fuel works. The fuel hookup, the venting and haul-away are all part of the job.

    Gas Tank Water Heater Installation in Providence

    A gas tank is the most common water heater we install, in 40, 50 and 75 gallon sizes. Each one gets a new gas connector, a sediment trap and a shutoff valve, plus a vent connector pitched up toward the flue at a quarter inch per foot. Then a draft test. Gas suits busy households.
