    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Warwick

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    Both are good choices. PEX usually costs less. PEX is flexible, handles freezing and movement better and installs faster, while copper is rigid, tolerates heat and sunlight and has a long track record, so the right answer depends on budget, water and preference.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Big houses need more time. The schedule depends on the number of fixtures, attic access and whether you choose PEX or copper, and water is usually back on at the end of each work day.

    It is not a good DIY project. Permits, testing and code all come into it. Mistakes inside walls lead to hidden leaks, failed inspections or a voided PEX warranty, and those can easily cost more than a licensed crew would charge for the whole job.

    Most policies do not pay for replacing old pipe, though they may cover sudden water damage. Some insurers raise rates or decline coverage for homes with polybutylene.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. We use Uponor most often. Copper replacements use Type L instead of thin Type M, since the heavier wall lasts longer.

    Both work well in Rhode Island homes. PEX bends, tolerates freezes and costs less. Copper is rigid, lasts a long time and tolerates sunlight and heat, and we will help you choose based on your home, your water and your budget.
