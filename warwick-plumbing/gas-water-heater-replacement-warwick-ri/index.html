    Tankless Gas Conversion in Warwick

    Going tankless usually means a bigger gas line, since a whole-house tankless unit can draw 199,000 BTU against the 40,000 a tank burner uses. The 1/2-inch line feeding a tank is rarely enough.     Rheem Gas Water Heater Replacement in Warwick, RI

    Rheem Performance and Performance Platinum tanks cover most of the range we install, from basic 6-year models to 12-year tanks with better anodes. The longer warranty usually means a bigger anode. We help you pick.

    Propane Gas Water Heater Replacement in Warwick, RI

    Propane heaters run at about 11 inches of water column instead of the 7 inches typical for natural gas, so the gas valve and orifice have to match the fuel. A natural gas unit does not go on propane unless the manufacturer allows a conversion. Pressure gets checked with a manometer.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Replacement in Warwick, RI

    Commercial gas heaters in restaurants and offices often run 75 to 100 gallons at 75,000 BTU or more, and they need a gas line sized for that load. Code is stricter here. We size by peak demand and recovery, not tank volume alone.

    Bradford White Tank Replacement in Warwick

    Most of the tanks on our trucks are Bradford White, partly because their Defender combustion system and ICON gas valve are simple to service. We help you match model to demand. The install meets current code.

    Choosing Between Gas Tank Brands in Warwick, RI

    Warranty length on a tank mostly tracks anode size and burner design, so a 12-year tank is a different build from a 6-year one. Some have self-cleaning dip tubes for sediment. Powered anodes outlast standard rods. We price it against how long you plan to stay.

    No Hot Water Calls in Warwick

    No hot water at all usually points to a dead thermocouple or a gas control valve that has failed after years of service. We check those first. On a tank past ten years, a new heater usually beats a new valve.

    Gas Water Heater Efficiency Ratings in Warwick

    Efficiency ratings on tank heaters are listed as UEF, uniform energy factor, and a higher number means less gas per gallon heated. A standard tank sits around 0.60 to 0.65. High-efficiency condensing tanks reach 0.80 or more and can qualify for rebates.
