    How We Keep Water Out of Walls in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Cumberland Hill

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, cement board with a membrane or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Cumberland Hill

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Basements are standard in most homes, and many rely on sump pumps to manage groundwater during wet seasons. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Foundation Crack Repair in Cumberland Hill

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin starting at the lowest port and working up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. We swap them for braided stainless hoses, put a drain pan under the washer piped to a floor drain where one is available, and install a quarter-turn shutoff or an automatic leak valve on the supply box. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Drainage System Integration in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. Downspouts get extended at least four feet out.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, six inches of drop over the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. Every option comes written out with its price.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Cumberland Hill

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that picks up the cooler wet area, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. The bad fitting or section of pipe gets replaced. Wet insulation comes out, and fans dry the cavity before we close it up.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Salt air near the coast can rust exposed metal fixtures, outdoor spigots and fittings faster than inland. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Cumberland Hill

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. A full exterior dig and membrane on a basement usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000, depending on depth, wall length and what sits against the house, while interior drain tile with a sump typically costs $4,000 to $12,000. Prices vary with access. We itemize every quote after the inspection.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, counting the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    Xypex, Tremco, Sika and Henry products show up on a lot of our jobs, covering crystalline products, elastomeric membranes and sealants. The product depends on the wall. Crystalline coatings work well on poured concrete, but block walls usually call for an elastomeric membrane or drainage board.

    Anywhere from an afternoon to over a week. Interior crack sealing might take a day or two, while exterior excavation with new footing drains might run several days or longer than a week depending on the size of the house. Rain delays exterior work.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, using drain tile under the slab edge and a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. Outside costs more.

    Most systems we install should go 20 to 30 years or longer. Life span comes down to how it was installed, what method was used and what the soil around the house is like. The sump pump will need replacing well before that. Most pumps last seven to ten years.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, generally more than about ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. Small patches we cut out and replace ourselves.

    Some handy homeowners do. Rolling on a liquid membrane is not hard, but seams, inside corners and the drain connection are where most leaks start, and a pan also needs a correct quarter-inch-per-foot slope plus a 24-hour flood test. A pro install lowers the odds of hidden damage.
