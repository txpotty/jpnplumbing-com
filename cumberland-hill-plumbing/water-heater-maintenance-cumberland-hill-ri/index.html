    What a Water Heater Tune-Up Covers in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Tankless Water Heater Service in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Tankless units need descaling. Mineral scale coats the inside of the heat exchanger, restricting flow and making the unit work harder, and eventually it starts throwing error codes or cutting out in the middle of a shower. A pump circulates descaler through it. We also check the igniter, the fan and the exhaust vent for safe operation.

    Descaling Tankless Units in Cumberland Hill

    Error codes often mean scale. Tankless heaters are especially sensitive to hard water, and once scale coats the heat exchanger, flow drops, the unit overheats and the control board shuts it down to protect itself. A pump kit handles it. Most makers recommend descaling at least yearly with hard water.

    Relief Valve and Safety Testing in Cumberland Hill, RI

    The relief valve is the tank's last line of defense. We lift the lever, confirm water discharges and the valve reseats without dripping, and check that the discharge pipe runs full size to an approved spot within a few inches of the floor or outside. We swap bad ones. Expansion tanks get an air charge check.

    Gas Water Heater Safety Checks in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Gas units need a clean burner and a good draft. We check for gas leaks at the valve and connector, watch the flame, clean the burner and confirm the flue pulls exhaust up and out instead of spilling it into the room. Carbon monoxide is the concern. We also test the thermocouple.

    Commercial Tank and Tankless Service in Cumberland Hill, RI

    Commercial heaters work harder than home units. Large tanks and multi-unit tankless racks see constant demand, so scale and wear build faster, and they need flushing, descaling and component checks done to the manufacturer's commercial schedule.     Commercial Water Heater Service in Cumberland Hill

    Anode Rod Checks in Cumberland Hill

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. It is a long metal rod screwed into the top of the tank, and once it has corroded down to bare wire, the water starts eating the steel tank lining instead. We pull it and look. Replacing the rod is much cheaper than replacing the heater.
