    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in East Providence, RI

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. Busy kitchens need them cleaned every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.

    Preventive Maintenance Programs in East Providence, RI

    A maintenance plan is just a pumping interval plus someone who tracks it. We keep the calendar so your manager does not have to. If the sludge judge shows the trap hitting 20 percent between visits, we pull the next date in, and if it reads 10 percent, we push it out and save you a pump-out. Adjustments happen at every visit.

    Grease Trap Inspection and Repair in East Providence

    An empty trap is the only time anyone can see what is wrong with it. Cracked baffles and missing outlet tees are the usual finds. Without an outlet tee, the grease cap flows right into the sewer, and older concrete interceptors lose that tee more often than you would think. We can often fix small problems on the spot.

    Residential Grease Trap Cleaning in East Providence, RI

Service means scooping out grease and solids by hand, cleaning the baffle, and seating the gasket so the lid seals tight. About thirty minutes.

    Grease Trap Cleaning for Restaurants in East Providence, RI

    Grease Trap Hydro-Jetting in East Providence, RI

    Some traps have years of grease baked onto the walls and inlet piping. That crust survives a normal pump-out. Jetting the empty tank and its inlet line strips it back to bare walls and gets back the capacity that crust was taking. Plan on about an extra hour.

    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in East Providence, RI

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly. A 1,500-gallon interceptor that has gone five months without pumping needs a vacuum truck, a pressure wand and extra disposal fees for the volume, so it lands at the high end. Regular pumping keeps the per-visit price down.

    Grease Line Jetting in East Providence

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. It builds up in rings, thickest at the bottom of the pipe. Snaking just bores a small path that closes up in weeks, but a rotating jetter nozzle strips grease off the full wall of the pipe. A camera confirms it afterward.
