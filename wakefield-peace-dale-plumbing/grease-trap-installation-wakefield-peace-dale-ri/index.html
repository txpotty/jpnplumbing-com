    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Wakefield-Peace Dale

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. A floor-recessed trap costs more than one set on the floor. Location drives the rest. Lids under traffic cost more, and so does repaving.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. Strictly, a grease trap is the small indoor unit rated in gallons per minute, and an interceptor is the large tank rated in gallons of capacity. Codes vary on the wording. Many codes say hydromechanical interceptor and gravity interceptor instead, and we match the local wording on the permit.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. Our time on site is usually one day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. We like the plastic bodies from Schier where steel would rust out. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.

    You get the manufacturer's warranty on the unit and a written labor warranty from us on the installation. We register the unit with the maker so the factory warranty is on record. Keep your cleaning logs. Makers may ask for service records before paying a claim.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We add up the drain rate of each sink compartment, floor sink and dish machine, then apply the code's drain-down time to get gallons per minute. Gravity tanks use volume instead. For those, the local pretreatment program often has its own formula based on seats or fixture units, and we use theirs.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. We put our labor warranty in writing on the invoice, covering leaks at the joints we made and any problem with how we set and leveled the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.
